# Style Directions

Use these as lenses, not costumes. Each direction has a logic, a risk, and a set of moves. Choose one primary direction and at most one accent influence.

## Editorial

**Logic:** content is the product. The page should feel authored, not assembled.

**Moves:**
- Strong display type with a clear scale.
- Generous margins and asymmetric layouts.
- Real photography or illustration with art direction.
- Pull quotes, captions, and metadata treated as design elements.
- A restrained palette with one strong accent.

**Risks:** slow scanning, dense mobile layouts, type that overwhelms content.

**Avoid:** fake newspaper rules, tiny uppercase eyebrows on every section, and “broadsheet” layouts that ignore reading flow.

## Swiss / International

**Logic:** clarity through grid, alignment, and typographic discipline.

**Moves:**
- Strong grid, left alignment, generous whitespace.
- One or two sans-serif families.
- Limited palette, often black, white, and one accent.
- Objective photography or diagrams.
- Clear hierarchy through size and weight.

**Risks:** cold, generic, low personality; can look like a template.

**Avoid:** pretending a grid is a concept. The content still needs a point of view.

## Minimal

**Logic:** remove everything that does not help the task.

**Moves:**
- Few colors, few weights, lots of space.
- One primary action per screen.
- Precise alignment and spacing.
- Subtle borders or shadows only where needed.
- Strong empty states.

**Risks:** can feel empty, unfinished, or luxury-only; hides complexity instead of organizing it.

**Avoid:** minimalism as an excuse for missing states or weak content.

## Warm humanist

**Logic:** technology should feel approachable and human.

**Moves:**
- Warm neutrals, soft contrast, rounded shapes.
- Friendly sans or humanist serif.
- Organic illustrations, hands, faces, real environments.
- Comfortable line height and spacing.
- Gentle motion.

**Risks:** can feel childish, untrustworthy, or generic “wellness.”

**Avoid:** rounded cards plus pastel gradients plus stock smiling people with no product specificity.

## Technical / utilitarian

**Logic:** the user is capable; density and precision are features.

**Moves:**
- Compact spacing, strong alignment, tabular data.
- Monospace for code, IDs, and numeric data.
- Clear states, keyboard shortcuts, command palette.
- Restrained color; status colors are meaningful.
- Documentation-like clarity.

**Risks:** hostile to newcomers, cluttered, poor mobile.

**Avoid:** monospace everywhere, fake terminal aesthetics, and density without hierarchy.

## Brutalist / neo-brutalist

**Logic:** expose the medium; reject polish as a default.

**Moves:**
- Raw type, hard borders, high contrast.
- Deliberate asymmetry and visible structure.
- Limited palette, often black/white plus one loud color.
- Minimal radius, visible grids, raw imagery.

**Risks:** accessibility, readability, and trust; can be a costume.

**Avoid:** using brutalism to excuse broken layout, low contrast, or unreadable type.

## Glass / tactile

**Logic:** depth, material, and atmosphere.

**Moves:**
- Translucent surfaces with backdrop blur.
- Layered gradients and soft highlights.
- Fine borders and inner shadows.
- Realistic material cues: glass, paper, metal, fabric.
- Restrained motion that reinforces depth.

**Risks:** contrast failures, GPU cost on low-end devices, trend fatigue.

**Avoid:** blurring everything and calling it premium. Blur is not hierarchy.

## Bento

**Logic:** modular cells that let different content types coexist.

**Moves:**
- A grid of cells with varied sizes.
- One hero cell, several supporting cells.
- Clear internal hierarchy in each cell.
- Consistent spacing and alignment.
- Responsive reflow that preserves priority.

**Risks:** becomes a wall of identical cards; hides weak content behind layout.

**Avoid:** making every cell the same size and calling it a system.

## Maximalist / collage

**Logic:** abundance, energy, and cultural specificity.

**Moves:**
- Layered imagery, texture, and type.
- Mixed scales and unexpected crops.
- Saturated color and pattern.
- Deliberate imperfection.
- Strong art direction to keep it coherent.

**Risks:** cognitive overload, performance, accessibility, mobile collapse.

**Avoid:** chaos without hierarchy. Maximalism still needs a reading order.

## Retrofuturist

**Logic:** nostalgia for a future that never arrived.

**Moves:**
- Chrome, neon, pixel, CRT, and sci-fi references.
- High contrast and dramatic type.
- Motion that evokes old interfaces or new worlds.
- A tight palette and consistent era.

**Risks:** dates quickly; can feel gimmicky.

**Avoid:** mixing five eras. Pick one reference world.

## Organic / anti-grid

**Logic:** human, natural, fluid.

**Moves:**
- Soft shapes, curves, and gradients.
- Asymmetric flow and overlapping elements.
- Earthy or botanical palettes.
- Calm, slow motion.
- Real textures and natural imagery.

**Risks:** responsive breakage, CLS, low contrast, vague hierarchy.

**Avoid:** blobs as a substitute for structure.

## Choosing a direction

Ask:

1. What is the product's world? (materials, language, rituals, tools)
2. What should the user feel: calm, capable, curious, premium, rebellious?
3. What does the content demand: density, imagery, reading, comparison?
4. What constraints dominate: accessibility, performance, brand, regulation?
5. What would be the most obvious, generic choice? Avoid it unless it is genuinely best.

The best direction is the one that makes the content feel inevitable.
